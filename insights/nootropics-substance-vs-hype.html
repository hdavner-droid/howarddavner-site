Nootropics: Substance vs. Hype

By Howard Davner · Co-Founder, NERD Focus · June 2026

"Nootropic" has become one of the most overused words on a beverage label. It signals focus, clarity, brainpower — and increasingly it's slapped onto products that don't earn it. As co-founder of NERD Focus, I, Howard Davner, work in this category every day, and I think consumers deserve a clearer way to separate what's real from what's marketing.

What a nootropic actually is

Broadly, a nootropic is any ingredient intended to support some aspect of cognitive function — attention, alertness, mental stamina. That spans a wide range, from the familiar (caffeine and L-theanine) to a long list of compounds with varying degrees of evidence behind them. The category isn't snake oil, but it isn't magic either. The honest position is somewhere in the middle: some ingredients have meaningful support, others are early or thin, and dosage matters enormously.

The dosage problem

Here's the detail most labels gloss over: an ingredient can be real and still be useless in the amount provided. A drink can list an impressive-sounding compound while including a fraction of the dose any study actually used. This is the most common gap between substance and hype — not fake ingredients, but real ingredients at "fairy dust" levels included mainly so they can appear on the label. When I evaluate a formulation, the first question is always whether the amounts are functional, not just present.

How to read a focus drink honestly

A few practical filters help. Look for transparency on amounts, not just an ingredient list. Be skeptical of products promising dramatic, instant transformation — real cognitive support is usually subtle, not cinematic. And notice whether the brand explains its choices or just leans on buzzwords. A company confident in its formula tends to tell you what's in it and why; a company selling hype tends to tell you how amazing you'll feel.

Why the honest path wins

It would be easier, commercially, to overpromise. But functional beverages live or die on repeat purchase, and you cannot fake a benefit a customer doesn't actually feel. Overclaiming gets you one sale and a disappointed customer. Building something that genuinely does what it says — at doses that matter — is slower, but it's the only foundation for a brand that lasts. In a category drowning in hype, substance is the real competitive advantage.

The nootropics trend is real, and it isn't going away. The brands worth trusting will be the ones that respect the science enough to be honest about it.
