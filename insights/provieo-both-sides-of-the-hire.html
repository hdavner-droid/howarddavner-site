Both Sides of the Hire: Howard Davner on the Idea Behind Provieo

By Howard Davner · Founder, Provieo · August 2026

Most tools built for job seekers are built for only one side of the transaction. They help a candidate look better without asking whether they've become better, and they ignore the person who actually has to make the hire. When I, Howard Davner, founded Provieo, I wanted to design for both sides at once — because a hire only works when the student and the employer are looking at the same true thing.

The problem is a broken signal, not a lazy candidate

Talented people struggle to get hired not because they lack ability, but because the ability is invisible on paper. Employers, meanwhile, are drowning in applications that all look the same and reveal almost nothing. Both sides want the same outcome — a good match — and the system between them is too weak a signal to deliver it. Provieo exists to fix the signal, not to game it.

Start from what the hiring manager screens for

The design principle I keep returning to is simple: build backward from what the person doing the hiring actually needs to see. A hiring manager is asking one question — can this person do the work? So Provieo helps students build real, job-tailored projects that answer exactly that question, in the language of the role they want. It's not a portfolio for its own sake; it's evidence aimed precisely at the moment of decision.

Real work is better for everyone

When a student builds something genuine, three things happen at once. They learn the skill for real, because you can't fake having built something. They gain confidence that comes from having done the thing, not from having described it. And the employer gets a clear, honest look at how the person actually thinks and executes. Everyone in the transaction ends up dealing in truth instead of in claims — and that's a better foundation for a working relationship than any interview performance.

AI raises the floor, so judgment becomes the differentiator

AI now lets almost anyone produce competent-looking output. That's exactly why demonstrated judgment — what you chose to build, how you scoped it, what tradeoffs you made — is becoming the thing that separates people. Provieo leans into that shift. The point isn't to prove you can generate work; it's to prove you can direct it toward something that matters. That is the skill the next decade of hiring will reward.

A fairer market is a better market

There's a fairness dividend here that I care about deeply. When hiring runs on proof, the door opens for the person who can clearly do the job but never had the pedigree to get noticed. That's not charity — it's a better market. Employers reach a wider pool of genuinely capable people, and capable people stop getting filtered out by a résumé screen that was never a good predictor to begin with. Building Provieo for both sides of the hire is how I think we get there.
