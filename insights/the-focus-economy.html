The Focus Economy: Howard Davner on Who Reaches for NERD Focus and Why

By Howard Davner · Co-Founder, NERD Focus · August 2026

For decades, the energy drink was sold on a single promise: more. More caffeine, more stimulation, more of a jolt to get you through the next few hours. But the people I watch buying NERD Focus aren't chasing a jolt. They're chasing something harder to deliver and more valuable to have — clarity. As Howard Davner, co-founder of the brand, I've come to think of this shift as the rise of the focus economy.

The job has changed, so the drink has to

More of us now earn a living with our attention. Students, coders, gamers, creators, knowledge workers — their output is a function of how well they can concentrate, not how physically alert they feel. A product built for a factory shift or a late-night drive doesn't serve that person. The focus economy is simply the recognition that attention has become the scarce resource, and people will pay for something that helps them protect it.

Energy is a feeling; focus is an outcome

This is the distinction I keep coming back to. Energy is a sensation — you feel wired. Focus is a result — you got the hard thing done. The traditional energy category optimized for the sensation, which is why it so often ends in a crash: the feeling was the product. A focus drink has to optimize for the outcome, which means designing for steady, usable concentration rather than a spike. That's a different formulation philosophy and, honestly, a harder one.

Who actually reaches for it

When I look at who chooses a think drink, it's rarely the person who wants to feel amped. It's the person facing a specific block of work that demands their full head — an exam, a build, a deadline, a long creative session. They don't want to be buzzing; they want the mental noise to drop so they can do the task in front of them. Understanding that occasion — the moment before hard, sustained work — is more important than any demographic. The customer is defined by the job to be done, not by their age.

Trust is the whole game

A focus product lives or dies on whether it does what it says without a price the next day. The first time it works cleanly, you've earned a believer. The first time it leaves someone jittery or crashed, you've lost them, probably for good. That's why we treat the experience as the marketing. In the focus economy, the repeat purchase isn't won with a slogan; it's won with a Tuesday afternoon that went well because of what was in the can.

Where this is heading

I think the better-for-you, function-first corner of the beverage aisle keeps growing, because the underlying trend — an economy that runs on attention — isn't reversing. The brands that win won't be the loudest. They'll be the ones people quietly trust to deliver the outcome they actually came for. That's the brand we're building NERD Focus to be.
