What Your Old Comic Books Are Really Worth

By Howard Davner · Touchstone Gold and Silver · November 2026

Almost every estate we see has a box of comics in it: in a closet, under a bed, in the attic of a parent's house. Families usually arrive with one of two beliefs. Either the box is worthless, or it's a lottery ticket. The truth is almost always in between, and it depends on a handful of things anyone can learn to check. I'm Howard Davner, and at Touchstone Gold and Silver we buy comics from collectors and families. Here is what actually drives value.

The headlines are real, but they're the exception

The top of the market is extraordinary. A copy of Action Comics #1, the first appearance of Superman, sold for $15 million in January 2026, and a Superman #1 graded CGC 9.0 sold for $9.12 million at Heritage Auctions in 2025. Those books are 85 years old and survive in tiny numbers. The same hobby that produces those sales also produces millions of issues worth less than the paper they're printed on, so the question is always which kind of book you're holding.

What makes a comic valuable

1. Age. Collectors group books into eras: the Golden Age (about 1938 to 1956), the Silver Age (1956 to about 1970) and the Bronze Age that followed. Older books survive in smaller numbers, especially in good condition.

2. Key issues. A first appearance, an origin story or a #1 issue can be worth many times the issue next to it. Most of a book's value often comes from what happens inside it, not the cover.

3. Condition. This is where most of the money is won or lost. Comics are graded on a ten-point scale, and at the high end small differences matter enormously. One widely cited example: an Amazing Spider-Man #300 is worth about $110 at a CGC 8.0 and more than $2,400 at a CGC 9.8.

4. Authenticity and originality. Reprints and facsimile editions copy famous covers and are worth a fraction of the originals. Trimmed edges, restoration and missing pages all reduce value, sometimes sharply.

Graded or raw?

Third-party graders such as CGC and CBCS seal a book in a case with a numeric grade, and PSA entered comic grading in 2025, adding competition to a field CGC had long dominated. A graded book is easier to price because the condition question is settled. But grading costs money and time, so it only makes sense for books that are valuable enough to justify it. A good buyer will tell you honestly whether a raw key issue is worth grading before you decide to sell.

Where the market stands in 2026

Comic prices ran up sharply during 2020 to 2022 and then fell back as that bubble burst. Market trackers describe 2025 and 2026 as a period of stabilization, with high-grade Silver Age keys holding or slightly rising while speculative modern books gave back gains. The lesson for families: historically important books in strong condition have held up best.

How to sell a collection without being shortchanged

  • Don't clean, press or remove books from their bags. Well-meaning cleanup can reduce value.
  • Look for keys first. Pull out anything pre-1985, any #1 issue, and anything you can identify as a first appearance.
  • Ask how each offer was reached. A fair buyer prices key books one by one against recent sales of the same issue in similar condition, and prices modern bulk as a lot. They should be able to show you the comparison.
  • Get it in writing, with no pressure. You should be able to take an offer home and think about it.
  • Know the alternatives. For a truly major key, a specialist auction may bring more money, with fees and a wait. For a mixed collection, a direct sale is faster and simpler. Either can be the right answer.

It's the same principle I apply to gold: the seller should understand the process before anything is bought. An informed seller makes a better decision, whatever they decide.
