Why Gold Prices Are Falling

By Howard Davner · Touchstone Gold and Silver · November 2026

At our buying sessions this month, the most common question hasn't been "what is this worth?" It has been "why did the price drop?" It's a fair question. Gold climbed to an intraday record of about $5,595 an ounce on January 29, 2026, and by mid-November it had fallen to its lowest level in more than a year, trading around $3,830 on November 16. I'm Howard Davner, and I spent more than a decade in finance before building Touchstone Gold and Silver. Here is my plain-English read on what's behind the move.

How big is the drop?

It's large by gold's standards. Based on London's afternoon benchmark price, gold fell 15.1% over the twelve weeks ending November 13, one of its five steepest twelve-week declines in twenty years, and the price fell in ten of those twelve weeks. That came after a similar decline over the summer.

Reason 1: Higher interest rates make gold less attractive to hold

Gold pays no interest. When bond yields rise, the "cost" of holding gold instead of an interest-paying asset goes up, and some investors move money out of it. In November, a hotter-than-expected U.S. inflation report and hawkish comments from Federal Reserve officials pushed expectations toward higher rates, not lower ones. That is a headwind for gold.

Reason 2: A stronger dollar

Gold is priced in U.S. dollars worldwide. When the dollar strengthens, gold becomes more expensive for buyers paying in other currencies, which tends to soften demand and the price. Analysts tied this month's drop partly to the dollar's rally toward a 19-month high.

Reason 3: Investors selling gold to cover losses elsewhere

When stock markets fall hard, investors sometimes sell what they can sell, and gold is easy to sell. A sell-off in AI-related stocks this fall pushed some investors to sell gold to cover losses in equities. Nvidia alone lost about $1.9 trillion in market value between early October and mid-November, which added to the pressure.

What hasn't changed

Not every buyer is leaving. Central banks bought 321 tonnes of gold in the third quarter, according to World Gold Council figures, and refiners report that physical demand picked up after the sharp November drop as buyers who had waited out the rally stepped in. Markets move in both directions; a falling price is a change in conditions, not a verdict on gold.

What it means if you're thinking about selling jewelry

I don't make price predictions, and nobody can tell you where gold will be next month. What I can tell you is how the price affects you as a seller. Scrap-gold offers are tied to that day's metal price, so the same chain is worth less today than it was in January. If you decide to sell, the fundamentals are the same in any market: understand the purity and weight of what you have, know the day's spot price, and work with a buyer who tests and weighs in front of you and explains how the offer was calculated. I wrote more about that in Know the Process Before You Sell Your Gold.

And if you're not in a hurry, there is no rule that says you have to sell now. The best decision is an informed one.
